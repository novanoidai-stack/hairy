One of the attractive features of phase plane analysis is that there is a direct method to show the existence of limit cycles. The theorem of PoincarÃ©-Bendixson (204) tells us that, if (i) we can construct a bounding surface around a fixed point so that all flux arrows on the surface are pointing toward the interior, and (ii) the fixed point in the interior is repulsive (real part of both eigenvalues positive), then there must exist a stable limit cycle around that fixed point.

The proof follows from the uniqueness of solutions of differential equations which implies that trajectories cannot cross each other. If all trajectories are pushed away from the fixed point, but cannot leave the bounded surface, then they must finally settle on a limit cycle; cf. Fig.Â 4.9. Note that this argument holds only in two dimensions.
