3.2 Spatial Structure: The Dendritic Tree

Neurons in the cortex and other areas of the brain often exhibit highly developed dendritic trees that may extend over several hundred microns (Fig. 3.4). Synaptic input to a neuron is mostly located on its dendritic tree. Disregarding NMDA- or Calcium-based electrogenic âspikesâ, action potentials are generated at the soma near the axon hillock. Up to now we have discussed point neurons only, i.e., neurons without any spatial structure. What are the consequences of the spatial separation of input and output?

The electrical properties of point neurons have been described as a capacitor that is charged by synaptic currents and other transversal ion currents across the membrane. A non-uniform distribution of the membrane potential on the dendritic tree and the soma induces additional longitudinal current along the dendrite. We are now going to derive the cable equation that describes the membrane potential along a dendrite as a function of time and space. In SectionÂ 3.4 we will see how geometric and electrophysiological properties can be integrated in a comprehensive biophysical model.
